# Lyrical prose

A writing style that takes any plain English draft, or any bare theme, and renders it as flowing, contemplative prose, with sentences that move from a single concrete fact to a wide horizon, ground every abstraction in a plain image, and close on a note of quiet affirmation.

The style is derived and verified from the prose of Rabindranath Tagore in *Sādhanā: The Realisation of Life*. **The skill is generic.** It teaches the *mechanics* of the style, never the book's subject, religion, or vocabulary. Each rule carries a short anchor quote from the original as evidence of *how a device works*. The anchors are never content for you to reproduce. The full rules are under `./reference/`. Read the relevant file before applying or citing a rule.

**Never quote an anchor you have not loaded this session, and never invent a rule or a passage. If you have not read the file, read it first or say nothing.**

## Core mandate

Move the reader from a concrete particular to a wide horizon, and let the prose breathe like music. When transmuting a draft, preserve its argument and facts exactly, and change only register, rhythm, framing, and imagery. Ornament never stands on its own, because every image serves a thought.

## House style

The finished prose follows a strict punctuation rule. Inside and between sentences, use only commas and periods. No em dashes, no colons, no semicolons. Where a colon or semicolon would join two clauses, split them into two sentences or join them with a comma and a conjunction. A question mark may close a rhetorical question, nothing more. Let each paragraph run as continuous, flowing prose from end to end, with no hard line breaks and no short, split lines.

## Style DNA

> Begin in the dust of a single fact and end at a wide horizon. Set two opposites against each other and let them dissolve into harmony. Ground every abstraction in a seed, a wave, a river, or a rainy night. Let long sentences gather and short ones strike. Keep every word plain English, affirm what endures, and close on a note of joy.

## Confirmation

When you are only reviewing or advising, report findings without pausing. Before rewriting an existing file, confirm the change set first, and ask only for what you cannot infer, the theme, the intent, and how far to push the register (light touch or full voice), one item at a time.

## Decision sequence

Work every piece through these steps in order.

1. **Frame the request.** Transmute an existing draft or compose new prose? Settle theme, intent, and register dial. Refer to [reference/transmute-vs-compose.md](reference/transmute-vs-compose.md).
2. **Find the particular.** Choose the single concrete fact or object the piece will rise from. Refer to [reference/structure-and-movement.md](reference/structure-and-movement.md).
3. **Set the poles.** Choose the two opposites the piece will reconcile. Refer to [reference/structure-and-movement.md](reference/structure-and-movement.md).
4. **Choose the images.** Pick two to four motifs grounded in plain, sensory things. Refer to [reference/imagery-and-metaphor.md](reference/imagery-and-metaphor.md).
5. **Draft the arc.** Move from the particular to the universal to the wide horizon, closing on affirmation. Refer to [reference/structure-and-movement.md](reference/structure-and-movement.md).
6. **Tune the cadence.** Alternate long and short sentences, then add parallelism and anaphora. Refer to [reference/cadence-and-syntax.md](reference/cadence-and-syntax.md).
7. **Set the diction.** English only, sparing absolutes, aphoristic closes. Refer to [reference/diction-and-register.md](reference/diction-and-register.md).
8. **Set voice and devices.** Fix the tone, then add rhetorical questions, address, and optional quote-and-gloss. Refer to [reference/voice-and-tone.md](reference/voice-and-tone.md) and [reference/rhetoric-and-address.md](reference/rhetoric-and-address.md).
9. **Run the checklist.** Refer to [reference/checklist.md](reference/checklist.md).

## Output

- When **composing or transmuting**, return the finished prose, then a short note on the choices made, the particular, the poles, the motifs, so the user can steer.
- When **reviewing**, return each finding as the quoted line, the problem in a few words, a rewrite, and a one-line rationale. Group repeated issues and lead with the highest-impact ones.

## Anti-patterns

- Purple pastiche, ornament with no argument underneath.
- Uniform long sentences with no short ones to break them, which loses the music.
- Any Sanskrit, foreign, or untranslated term, or borrowed words left unglossed.
- Scattering capitalised absolutes until they lose weight.
- Em dashes, colons, or semicolons in the finished prose, or text broken into short, line-split fragments.
- Anachronistic, mechanical, or corporate imagery that breaks the register.
- Polemic or contempt in place of serene contrast.
- Distorting the source draft's facts to fit the voice when transmuting.
- Forcing scripture or a religious frame onto a subject that did not call for it.
- Quoting an anchor or citing a rule not grounded in the reference files.
