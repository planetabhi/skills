# Transmute vs. compose

The two tasks share one style engine. The difference is where the content comes from and what must be protected.

## Transmute, recast an existing draft

Take the user's draft, on any subject, and render it in the lyrical style.

- **Protect the content.** Keep every claim, fact, number, and conclusion exactly as given. Change only register, rhythm, framing, and imagery.
- **Map, don't invent.** Find the draft's own thesis and lay it along the particular to horizon arc. The opening particular and the reconciled poles should come from the draft's own material.
- **Flag, don't distort.** If a claim cannot survive the lyrical voice without being bent or exaggerated, say so and leave it plain, rather than falsifying it for effect.
- **Honour the register dial.** A light touch keeps most of the user's own words and adds warmth and cadence. The full voice carries the complete arc and the lifted close.

## Compose, generate new prose

Start from the user's theme and build the piece.

1. Find the **concrete particular** to open on, a fact or object that belongs to the theme.
2. Choose the **pair of reconciled opposites** the piece will turn on.
3. Select **two to four image motifs**, drawn from the theme's own world.
4. Draft the **arc**, from the particular to the universal to the wide horizon.
5. **Close on an affirmation**, short and lifted.

## Do / Don't

- **Do**, when transmuting, treat the facts as fixed points the prose must flow around.
- **Do**, when composing, let the theme supply its own particulars and images.
- **Don't** add claims the draft did not make, or quietly drop ones it did, in the name of flow.
- **Don't** reach outside the theme for a borrowed grandeur, a foreign term, a scripture, an image from another world.

## After either task

Return the finished prose, then a short note naming the choices made, the particular, the poles, the motifs, so the user can steer the next pass. Then run [checklist.md](checklist.md).
