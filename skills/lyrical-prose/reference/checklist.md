# Checklist

Run this against every finished piece. Each item is pass or fail, and a lyrical piece should pass all of them.

## Structure and movement
- [ ] Opens on a concrete particular before widening out.
- [ ] Turns on at least one reconciled pair of opposites.
- [ ] Closes on a short, exalted affirmation, and the piece lands rather than trailing off.
- [ ] Each paragraph lets a single image carry it, rather than crowding several.

## Cadence
- [ ] Long and short sentences alternate, and nothing is a uniform stretch of either.
- [ ] Parallelism, antithesis, or anaphora appears where two ideas belong together, but no device is overused.

## House style
- [ ] The prose uses only commas and periods, with no em dashes, colons, or semicolons.
- [ ] Each paragraph flows end to end as continuous prose, with no hard line breaks or short, split lines.

## Imagery
- [ ] Every abstraction is tied to a plain, sensory image.
- [ ] Images come from nature and common life, not from machines, corporations, or software.
- [ ] No image contradicts or clutters the thought it serves.

## Diction
- [ ] Diction is entirely English, and no untranslated or borrowed term is left unglossed.
- [ ] Capitalised absolutes are used sparingly, not scattered.
- [ ] The register is elevated but not precious, with no jargon, buzzwords, or slang.

## Voice and fidelity
- [ ] The tone is serene and affirming, never polemical or contemptuous.
- [ ] For a transmute, every claim and fact of the source draft is preserved. For a compose, the theme is honoured and nothing foreign is forced on it.
- [ ] No scripture or religious frame is imposed on a subject that did not call for it.
- [ ] Reads as sincere lyrical prose, not as parody of the style.

## If reviewing
Report each failure as the quoted line, the problem in a few words, a rewrite, and a one-line rationale. Group repeated issues and lead with the highest-impact ones. Do not restate lines that already work.
