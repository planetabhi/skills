# Diction and register

Word choice. **English only, no Sanskrit, no foreign or untranslated vocabulary.** The register is elevated but plain, never precious.

## The register

- **Balance the Latinate and the Anglo-Saxon.** Pair abstract words (realisation, consciousness, comprehension) with short, plain ones, so the line stays grounded.
  - Anchor from chapter 2, "To understand anything is to find in it something which is our own."
- **Capitalised absolutes, used sparingly.** Truth, Beauty, the Infinite, the All are a plain English device for lifting a noun to its largest sense. Use at most one or two in a piece.
  - Anchor from chapter 2, "Facts are many, but the truth is one."
- **A faint archaic or biblical lift is allowed**, rarely, where it elevates, but it must never tip into costume.
- **Close on an aphorism.** End a movement on a short, weighty, self-contained line.
  - Anchor from chapter 1, "our possessions are our limitations."
  - Anchor from chapter 4, "It is the function of religion not to destroy our nature but to fulfil it."

## Do / Don't

- **Do** keep every word English and known to the reader.
- **Do** reserve capitalised absolutes for the one or two moments that carry the piece's weight.
- **Do** earn an aphoristic close by building to it, not by stapling a maxim on the end.
- **Don't** use Sanskrit or any untranslated term. If a rare or borrowed word is truly unavoidable, gloss it plainly in the same sentence.
- **Don't** scatter capitalised words. When everything is Capitalised, nothing is weighted.
- **Don't** reach for jargon, buzzwords, slang, or clinical modern abstraction, which shatter the register.
- **Don't** let "elevated" become "precious". If a plainer word says it, use the plainer word.
