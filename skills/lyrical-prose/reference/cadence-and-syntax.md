# Cadence and syntax

Sentence music, the most imitable and the most easily abused layer. The aim is a line that gathers and then releases, so the prose moves like breathing.

## The core rhythm

**Alternate long and short.** Let a long periodic sentence build through stacked clauses and suspended resolution, then let a short declarative land like a bell. The contrast is the music, and neither length alone makes it.

- Short strike, from chapter 2, "Facts are many, but the truth is one."
- Short strike, from chapter 3, "It laughs, dances and plays, it builds, hoards and loves in death's face."
- Long build, from chapter 1, "He felt that truth is all-comprehensive, that there is no such thing as absolute isolation in existence, and the only way of attaining truth is through the interpenetration of our being into all objects."

## Devices

- **Antithesis.** Set two halves against each other in one line.
  - Anchor from chapter 1, "His aim was not to acquire but to realise…"
- **Anaphora.** Repeat an opening to drive a rhythm, then resolve.
  - Anchor from chapter 1, "that man can think… that he can use… that in the long run…"
- **Triads and balanced pairs.** Group in threes or twos for cadence.
  - Anchor from chapter 1, "to be truly united in knowledge, love, and service with all beings."
- **Paired question and answer.** Pose a short question, answer in a longer breath.
  - Anchor from chapter 3, "The river has its boundaries, its banks, but is a river all banks?"

## Do / Don't

- **Do** vary sentence length deliberately. After a long build, cut short.
- **Do** let clauses accumulate toward a release, not sprawl without end.
- **Do** use parallel structure when two ideas belong together, so they sit in the same shape.
- **Do** build rhythm with commas and periods alone. Where a colon or semicolon would join two clauses, split them into two sentences or join them with a comma and a conjunction.
- **Don't** write run-ons that never resolve. Suspension without landing exhausts the reader.
- **Don't** write a stretch of uniformly short, choppy sentences. With no long breath among them the music dies.
- **Don't** use em dashes, colons, or semicolons. They are outside the house style.
- **Don't** force a device into every line. Rhythm comes from contrast, not saturation.
