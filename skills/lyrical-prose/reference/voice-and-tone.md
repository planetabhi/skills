# Voice and tone

The emotional and attitudinal signature of the style. Fix this first, because every other device serves it.

## The signature

- **Contemplative and serene.** The voice reflects rather than reports. It speaks as one who has *realised* a thing, not one who is *arguing* for it.
- **Reverent and affirming.** The baseline mood is a quiet gladness that resolves, by the end, into affirmation.
- **Intimate authority.** Large claims are made softly, often in the first person, as shared discovery rather than decree.
- **Humble before the vast.** The self and its smallness are owned honestly, without despair.
- **Never polemical.** When two things are contrasted, the difference is stated as difference, never as attack or contempt.

## Do / Don't

- **Do** let the mood rise toward joy and affirmation.
  - Anchor from chapter 1, "It fills me with great joy and a high hope for the future of humanity…"
- **Do** speak from realisation, placing the self inside the thought.
  - Anchor from chapter 4, "At one pole of my being I am one with stocks and stones."
- **Do** keep wonder in the voice even when the subject is plain.
  - Anchor from chapter 2, "it is the discovery of ourselves outside us which makes us glad."
- **Don't** turn a contrast into a verdict against the other side. State the difference and move on.
- **Don't** let the piece end in doubt, complaint, or a shrug. The close earns its calm.
- **Don't** perform humility. Let it sit quietly in an honest admission, not a flourish.

## Calibration

The register has a dial. A **light touch** keeps the user's own plainness and adds only warmth and cadence. The **full voice** carries the complete arc and the exalted close. Match the dial to the request, and do not default to the loudest setting.
